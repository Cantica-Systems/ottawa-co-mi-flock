ALPR delivers objective images of vehicles, which can lead to investigative breakthroughs through retrospective searches after incidents are reported.  It also offers real-time alerts for stolen vehicles or license plates, vehicles linked to wanted persons, missing or endangered individuals and other critical public safety issues.

Since the implementation of ALPR technology, HDPS has experienced a significant positive impact on investigations and overall crime reduction efforts.  Leads generated by ALPR have been instrumental in identifying and slowing down criminal activity, recovering stolen vehicles and property, supporting investigations into thousands of dollars in reported losses, solving cases that might otherwise have remained unresolved, and locating missing or endangered individuals in Holland City, across Michigan, and throughout the surrounding region.

Criminal activities, stolen vehicles, and missing persons cases often cross jurisdictional lines.  Therefore, responsible sharing of information with authorized law enforcement partners enables agencies to connect evidence, identify related criminal activities, and respond more swiftly in urgent situations.

HDPS utilizes ALPR technology with clearly defined safeguards.  All searches shall include a reason for the search, offense type and include a valid criminal complaint number.  All system activity is logged and reviewed by the Captain of Investigations and an audit process is in place.  HDPS does not use ALPR information for civil immigration enforcement purposes.

This transparency portal offers the public access to HDPS's ALPR policies, including safeguards, data retention practices, and usage statistics.  Our goal is to showcase the public safety benefits of this technology while also ensuring accountability in its usage.

They do not record any movements or actions. (Written within our department policy as well)

Our cameras capture one moment in time and show the back of a vehicle and the license plate.

Our cameras also show date, time and location of the camera providing the photo.What the ALPR System Does Not DoDoes not record audio or video.
Does not show or provide any information about the occupants of the vehicle.
Does not run the license plate or registered owner through the Secretary of State.
Does not provide GPS data or tracking information.
Does not use facial recognition technology.
Does not continuously track an individual's movements.
Does not use data to issue traffic tickets.
Does not capture demographic data.
Does not capture cell phone information or IP address data.
Does not provide immigration information or status What the ALPR System Does DoThe System focuses on vehicles- not people.

Vehicles checked in the system are very limited and specifically selected based on information gathered by officers or detectives during law enforcement  investigations.

These Checks are case-driven and because a vehicle is associated with criminal activity, wanted suspects, or lost individuals.

Each check requires justification by entering a complaint number and law enforcement reason for the check into the system. (Policy strictly limits to law enforcement only)

Each check is completely tracked and able to be audited.What the ALPR System Does DoFrom January 1, 2026 through August 31, 2026 the percentage of searches in the system by HPD staff in comparison to the number of license plate reads by HPD cameras= .0187%.

An extremely high percentage of information comes into the system and is automatically deleted after 30 days without ever being viewed by anyone.

In addition, if you are not involved in any criminal activity- the chance of having your vehicle viewed drops to a substantially lower percentage.HPD ALPR PolicyAn officer must confirm that the vehicle is still stolen, wanted, or otherwise of interest before proceeding (absent exigent circumstances). Without confirmation, a vehicle cannot be stopped solely on an ALPR alert.

Officers shall visually verify that the license plate of interest matches identically with the image of the license plate captured.  Including both the alphanumeric characters of the license plate, state of issue, and vehicle descriptors before proceeding.

Because the ALPR alert relates to a vehicle plate and may not relate to the person operating the vehicle, officers need reasonable suspicion and/or probable cause to make an enforcement stop of any vehicle.

Our agency follows applicable laws and policies that govern the use of this technology, and we are committed to ensuring it is used responsibly and with appropriate safeguards, oversight, and accountability.
